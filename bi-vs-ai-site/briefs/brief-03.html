Berth window priority

Hutchison Ports ECT Rotterdam (synthetic calls) · On a congested day, which arriving vessels keep their berth window (PROTECT), which can move a few hours (FLEX) and which go to the back of the queue (SHIFT)?

SYNTHETIC DATA. Company-level values on this page are generated by a seeded script and anchored to public data (see the workbook’s Generation_Log and Source_Key). They illustrate the decision rule; they are not facts about the company.

Decision-maker

Berth planner at a deep-sea container terminal; replanned every 8-hour shift.

Data

Real anchor: PortWatch (7,045 container calls a year at Rotterdam), UNCTAD (3,151 TEU average ship). Generated: 19 calls with ETA deviation, boxes, transshipment, connection slack, yard stacks.

Method

Five criteria (window adherence, call size, connections at risk, service tier, yard pressure), min-max 0–100, weighted; PROTECT ≥ 60, SHIFT ≤ 35.

Result

5 PROTECT, 5 FLEX, 9 SHIFT on the generated day. The top call stays first under all five weightings, but the number protected swings from 3 to 9.

Data confidence Low   Result confidence Low

Where the real data lives

Terminal operating system (Navis N4, TOPS): calls, stowage plans (BAPLIE), yard stacks, connections. Carrier ETA messages and AIS positions.

Dashboard

Dashboard placeholder: with real data, this decision is better served by a BI tool

Live ETAs and stowage refresh continuously, are confidential, and are shared by planners and carriers who need one view and an audit trail. BI (or the TOS’s own dashboards) wins; the AI page can only prototype the rule.

A demo page on the synthetic data is easy to build, which is why it is left out: it would show the rule, not the decision a company would actually run.
