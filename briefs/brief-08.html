Road, rail or barge for export containers

Perfetti Van Melle, Breda · A course group project, and the starting point for this study · Should the plant’s maritime export containers move off road onto rail or barge, and through Rotterdam or Antwerp?

Decision-maker

Logistics manager at a manufacturer moving steady volumes; PVM Breda ships up to about 13,500 t of export containers a year (upper bound).

Data

60 inputs from 35 public sources: KiM/Panteia cost rates, CO2emissiefactoren.nl, Dutch and Flemish truck charges, OSRM / EuRIS / OpenRailRouting distances, terminal turnaround times.

Method

Door-to-door annual cost, hours per trip and CO2 per mode and corridor; min-max 0–100 per corridor; weighted by the user. Scope check: widening to all PVM lanes was tested and dropped (no public lane data).

Result

Rail wins Rotterdam (72.7 vs 50.0 at default weights, €21.6k a year cheaper than road, −38% CO2). Road and barge tie on Antwerp, where there is no rail service. Recommended: pilot the Rotterdam lane on the Tilburg–Rotterdam rail shuttle for one quarter.

Data confidence Moderate–high   Result confidence Moderate

BI vs AI for this decision

AI page is enough

A one-off corridor decision on public data, made by one manager. The Data Studio build worked, but it took workarounds (reshaping the model into a long table, parameter naming, filter quirks), and nothing in the decision needed a platform. A web page computing the model in the browser would have been quicker to build. Data Studio’s real advantage was that the team could change inputs in the Google Sheet without touching code.
