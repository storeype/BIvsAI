BI vs AI › Brief 2

Winning liner services for a mid-size port

Port of Marseille-Fos · Which trade lanes the port should court with incentives this year, which to hold, and which to concede to competing ports.

Decision-maker

Commercial director of a mid-size Mediterranean port competing with Genova, La Spezia, Barcelona and Valencia; yearly incentive plan.

Data

Eurostat maritime statistics (mar_go_am_fr/_it/_es): goods in large containers by port, partner country and direction, 2019–2025, pulled through the Eurostat API.

Method

47 partner lanes (≥250 kt across five gateways) scored on market size, growth, share gap vs Marseille’s 9.1% fair share, share momentum and lane balance; COURT ≥ 60, CONCEDE ≤ 35.

Result

14 lanes to court, 31 to hold, 2 to concede (Hong Kong, Lebanon). Morocco is the most robust target (top three under five of six weightings); China is already above fair share. The size of the COURT list (10–28) depends on the weights.

Data confidence High   Result confidence Moderate

Dashboard

Chart: Scatter. The question is where the prize is large and Marseille is under-represented. Market size against Marseille’s share, with its 9.1% fair share as a line, shows that directly; a ranked bar hid both reasons.

BI vs AI for this decision

AI page is enough

Annual public data for a yearly incentive plan fits an AI page. A BI tool would earn its place if the port joined its own call records, carrier bookings and incentive spend, refreshed monthly.
