Next steps: BI platform vs AI-built pipeline

Where the study goes from here: new problems built to favour BI on public data, why they still may not beat an AI-built pipeline, and how to test it properly.

Step 1: eighteen new problems for the static-page experiment

Brief 14 was the one public-data brief where BI won. These problems move its pattern into other domains. A problem qualifies if it meets C1 plus at least two other criteria. Confidence = confidence that it meets them. Data access is from the AI’s knowledge and still to be verified.

C1 Refresh (required)

Data updates weekly or faster and the decision recurs at that pace.

C2 Alerts

The value is in being told when a threshold is crossed.

C3 Many users · C4 Audit

Several roles act on one view; decisions are challenged later.

C5 Scale

Large volumes or several live feeds joined.

Tally: 6 high, 5 moderate–high, 6 moderate, 1 low–moderate. #7 and #17 overlap most with existing briefs; #13 is the most likely to go to an AI page. These problems were chosen to favour BI, the mirror image of the earlier selection bias.

Do they clear the stronger bar?

Mostly no. They were designed to beat a static page. An AI-built pipeline (a scheduled job that fetches the data, recomputes the model, rebuilds the page and sends alerts) closes most of that gap.

CriterionAI-built pipelineWhere BI still has an edge
C1 RefreshScheduled runs, hourly or fasterNone
C2 AlertsEmail or chat alerts in a few lines of codeNone
C4 Audit trailEvery run committed to git: timestamped and permanentNone; arguably better than most BI tools
C3 Many usersFixed questions, one viewSelf-service: non-coders asking new questions, slicing and drilling
C5 ScaleFine to tens of thousands of rowsHundreds of millions of rows or minute-level streams need a warehouse

Only #16 (volume), #10 (self-service across thousands of products) and perhaps #6 (joined live feeds) might still favour BI. Problems built around BI’s remaining edges (very large data, non-coder exploration, non-coder input editing, vendor-maintained connectors) are possible, but about 8–10 are genuinely distinct, not 18. That narrowness is itself a finding.

Proposed test

Brief 14 three ways

Static page, AI-built pipeline and a BI tool on the same PortWatch data. Daily data, twice-weekly decisions, alerts and an existing back-test make it the best head-to-head case. Prediction: the pipeline matches or beats BI unless several desks need to explore lanes themselves.

Brief 8 as the control

Low refresh, one decision-maker. Data Studio’s real advantage was letting non-coders edit inputs in a sheet; a page reading a published sheet tests whether that edge survives.

Not testable this way

Briefs 3, 4 and 15: their real versions need internal data.

Users served

1. Structural capacity (factual, no users needed): cost and effort from 1 to 10 to 100 users, per-user views and alerts, access control, whether a non-coder can answer a new question. 2. A small real test: 3–5 people complete the same tasks on both versions, timed. 3. Personas: useful for design, but hypothetical, so kept out of the verdict.
