Does AI make the BI tool unnecessary?

The hypothesis. If an AI can find the data, build the model and publish an interactive dashboard as a plain web page, the business-intelligence platform in the middle becomes unnecessary: the analyst no longer needs a BI tool to put a decision in front of a manager.

The test. We took the briefs on the course menu that public data can support, and ran each one to the standard of our main project (real company, Excel model, data map, themed deck), then asked for each: could a simple AI-built web dashboard serve the decision-maker, or does a BI tool add something the page cannot?

Answer: partly true. For four of six briefs an AI-built page is enough. A fifth needs a BI platform only once internal data comes in. The sixth genuinely needs one. The deciding factors are not the analysis, which AI handled in every case, but how often the data refreshes, whether the data is internal or sensitive, how many people act on it and whether decisions need an audit trail.

When does the BI tool earn its place?

Refresh cadence

Annual or quarterly public data: re-run the page. Daily or twice-weekly decisions (Brief 14): scheduled refresh is the product.

Internal data

Every brief got sharper with company data (terminal logs, purchase orders, sales by country, voyage records). Joining that data safely is what BI platforms do.

Many users

One manager deciding once a year needs a page. Several desks quoting one surcharge need governed definitions and access control.

Audit trail

Pricing and rerouting calls get challenged later. Keeping what the numbers said when the call was made is a platform feature, not a page feature.

What AI replaced in every brief: the BI developer’s work (finding sources, cleaning, modelling, building the visual). What it did not replace: the platform’s job of running the dashboard reliably for an organisation.

Open questions in this draft

Selection bias. We tested the briefs public data can reach, which are the most favourable cases for an AI-built page. The synthetic briefs (3, 4, 9, 13, 15, 18) depend on generated data and may change the answer.
Self-assessed confidence. The confidence ratings and the BI verdicts come from the same AI that built the models. An independent review (the team, or a separate check) should confirm them before they go in the report.
Reasoned, not measured. Apart from the main project (built in Data Studio), we did not build the BI-tool versions, so “BI adds value” is argued from the decision’s needs, not measured in time, cost or user outcomes.
No ground truth. Only Brief 14 has a back-test against what actually happened. The other recommendations are plausible, not validated.
The draft dashboards re-weight, they do not recompute. Scores are frozen when the page is built, so changing a price, threshold or dataset means editing the workbook and republishing the page. A BI tool connected to a live sheet only needs the sheet edited and a refresh. This is a limit of this draft, not of web pages: a page can also read a live published sheet or recompute the model in the browser, which would close the gap.
